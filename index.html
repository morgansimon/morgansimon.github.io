About Me

At The Hong Kong Polytechnic University, I am a PhD Candidate in the Department of Building Environment and Energy Engineering, supervised by Prof. Yaping Du and Prof. Mingli Chen. My doctoral research examines how complex electrical systems respond to fast electromagnetic disturbances as renewable generation becomes increasingly embedded in modern networks. It combines accelerated simulation methods with physically grounded models for lightning protection, cable electromagnetic compatibility, equipment behaviour, and photovoltaic power generation. The broader aim is to resolve transient phenomena across a wide range of frequencies and time scales, supporting safer and more reliable electrical system design. Before joining PolyU, I completed both an MSc and a BSc in Electrical Engineering at Wuhan University.

Research Interests

  • Accelerated methods for electromagnetic transient simulation in complex power systems, with emphasis on improving computational efficiency and numerical accuracy as interacting events evolve across multiple time scales.
  • Lightning transient analysis and protection design for distribution systems, accounting for lightning stroke effects, lightning-induced overvoltage, and the response of network equipment under severe electromagnetic disturbances.
  • Electromagnetic coupling in electrical cables, including the evaluation of shielding performance, grounding strategies, and high-frequency impedance for practical cable installations.
  • Broadband modelling of power-system equipment and low-voltage electrical safety under transient conditions, supporting a more reliable representation of equipment behaviour in system studies.
  • Renewable-energy applications, including photovoltaic power generation and grid integration, with attention to transient behaviour, power quality, and operational reliability.
